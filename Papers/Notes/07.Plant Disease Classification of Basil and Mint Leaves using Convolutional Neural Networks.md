## Title
Plant Disease Classification of Basil and Mint Leaves using Convolutional Neural Networks

---

# Basic Information

**Authors:** V. Sathiya, M. S. Josephine, V. Jeyabalaraja

**Journal:** International Journal of Intelligent Systems and Applications in Engineering (IJISAE)

**Year:** 2023

**Paper Type:** Research Paper

**Domain:**  
Computer Vision  
Deep Learning  
Plant Disease Classification  
Herbal Plant Disease Detection

---

# Why was this paper written?

The paper addresses the problem of automatically identifying diseases in basil and mint leaves.

Manual disease recognition is slow, depends on expert knowledge, and is difficult to scale for farmers and researchers.

The authors propose a CNN-based approach to classify basil and mint leaf diseases using InceptionV3.

---

# Main Contributions

1. Proposed a CNN-based classification system for basil and mint leaves.

2. Used InceptionV3 as the best-performing model.

3. Built a multi-class dataset for basil and mint disease classification.

4. Applied image preprocessing and augmentation to improve model learning.

5. Evaluated the model separately for basil and mint leaves.

---

# Dataset Used

The dataset contains both healthy and unhealthy basil and mint leaf images.

## Basil classes

- Healthy Basil
- Basil Mildew
- Basil Wilted

## Mint classes

- Healthy Mint Leaf
- Fusarium Wilt Mint Leaf
- Mint Leaf Rust
- Powdery Mildew Mint Leaf

### Dataset statistics

- Healthy Basil: 550 original images, 4500 total images, 320 used for validation
- Basil Mildew: 245 original images, 2120 total images, 158 used for validation
- Basil Wilted: 100 original images, 1280 total images, 80 used for validation
- Healthy Mint: 148 original images, 1510 total images, 150 used for validation
- Fusarium Wilt Mint: 220 original images, 2340 total images, 200 used for validation
- Mint Rust: 115 original images, 1420 total images, 110 used for validation
- Powdery Mildew Mint: 250 original images, 2050 total images, 185 used for validation

Total dataset size reported in the table: 15,220 images. 1

# Problem Statement

The paper tries to solve the problem of classifying basil and mint leaf diseases using image-based deep learning.

The authors focus on the need for:
- early disease detection,
- automatic classification,
- better support for farmers,
- and a model that can distinguish healthy and diseased herbal leaves.

---

# Data Collection

The images were collected from different places using a camera and from different sources. The paper also states that the images include both healthy and unhealthy leaves. 2

---

# Data Preprocessing

The paper mentions several preprocessing and augmentation steps:

- smoothing
- enhancement
- clipping
- noise reduction using a median filter
- preprocessing with the InceptionV3 preprocessing function
- real-time augmentation

### Augmentation techniques used

- vertical flip
- horizontal flip
- rotation range
- width shift
- height shift
- brightness variation

The paper says these were used to improve robustness and reduce the effect of noisy or low-quality images. 3

---

# Model Used

## InceptionV3

The authors tried multiple models and selected InceptionV3 as the best-performing model for both basil and mint classification. The model was trained from scratch with 2 fully connected layers at the end. 4

### Why InceptionV3?

The paper explains that the Inception module helps capture multi-scale features using different kernel sizes and pooling operations. This improves feature diversity and reduces overfitting by efficiently handling local and global patterns in leaf images. 5

---

# Training Details

- **Optimizer:** Adam
- **Batch size:** 32
- **Loss function:** Softmax classifier / classification loss
- **Training strategy:** trained till validation accuracy was achieved for basil and mint

The paper states that Adam was used with varied learning rates. 6

---

# Results

The reported performance was:

- **Basil validation accuracy:** 77.55%
- **Mint accuracy:** 70.89%

The paper presents these as the final validation results of the proposed InceptionV3-based model. 7

---

# Strengths

- Focuses on herbal plants, which are less covered than common crops.
- Uses a structured multi-class classification setup.
- Includes both healthy and diseased classes.
- Uses augmentation and preprocessing to improve model performance.
- Uses a known deep CNN architecture that is easy to understand and reproduce.

---

# Weaknesses

- The reported accuracy is moderate, not very high.
- The dataset is limited to basil and mint only.
- The paper does not use object detection or localization.
- It does not explain disease regions, only class labels.
- It is not directly suitable for real-time disease localization.
